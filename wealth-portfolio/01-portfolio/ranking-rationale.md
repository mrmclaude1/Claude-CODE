# Ranking Rationale — Why These Three Are the Portfolio Core

We are not building 22 businesses. We are building a **3-asset engine** plus opportunistic
add-ons. Here's the logic, structured as a private-equity operator would.

## The portfolio architecture (how the pieces fit)

```
        ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │  BUILD 1: Construction AI Automation Service  (the ENGINE)    │
        │  - Fastest cash flow (~1 mo payback)                          │
        │  - Funds everything else                                      │
        │  - Generates repeatable patterns + a client base             │
        └───────────────┬──────────────────────────┬──────────────────┘
                        │ patterns & demand          │ profit
                        ▼                            ▼
        ┌──────────────────────────────┐   ┌──────────────────────────────┐
        │ BUILD 2: Micro-SaaS (ASSET)  │   │ BUILD 3: Capital Portfolio    │
        │ - Productize the #1 pattern  │   │ - Index + dividend + crypto   │
        │ - Recurring revenue, exitable│   │ - Fully passive, compounding  │
        │ - 3.9–5x SDE exit value      │   │ - Where profit goes to rest   │
        └──────────────────────────────┘   └───────────────┬──────────────┘
                                                            ▼
                                            Real Estate → Acquisitions → FI
```

## Why Build 1 (Service) is the engine, not the SaaS

Counterintuitive but correct: **start with the service, not the software.**

- **Cash flow now.** Service revenue lands in weeks; SaaS takes months to find product-market
  fit. The service funds the SaaS instead of you funding it from savings.
- **Free market research.** Every client tells you exactly which workflow they'd pay to automate.
  You build the SaaS (Build 2) only after 3–5 clients pay you to solve the *same* problem manually.
  This converts SaaS from a guess into a backed bet — raising its probability of success.
- **Your moat is strongest here.** "AI automation agency" is crowded and generic. "The guy who ran
  $X0M in capital projects and now automates contractor back-offices" is a category of one in your
  local/regional market. The research is explicit: dissatisfaction with automation vendors is about
  *scope and change management* (61%, PwC), not tech — that's literally your PM skill set.
- **Low risk, low capital.** Under $300 to start (domain, email, a Stripe account). Payback in the
  first invoice.

**Confidence: 8/10.** The main risk is execution discipline on outreach, not market demand.

## Why Build 2 (Micro-SaaS) is the long-term asset

- **It compounds and it's sellable.** Service revenue stops if you stop. SaaS MRR is an asset that
  trades at 3.9–5× SDE (Acquire.com 2024–2025 median 3.9×, 71% margins). A $4k/mo product at 4× SDE
  ≈ a low-six-figure exit — built on patterns you already got *paid* to discover.
- **Highest automation ceiling (9/10).** Self-serve signup, AI-driven onboarding, usage-based
  billing — near-zero marginal time per customer once built.
- **De-risked by Build 1.** We only build it after the service proves the demand. That's why its
  probability (55%) is acceptable despite SaaS generally being hard.

**Confidence: 7/10** *conditional on Build 1 surfacing a repeatable, paid pattern first.*

## Why Build 3 (Capital Portfolio) is non-negotiable

- **It's the point of the whole exercise.** "Employment becomes optional" is a *net-worth* outcome,
  not a revenue outcome. Businesses convert to liquid, compounding assets here.
- **Truly passive (1 hr/mo, 10/10 automation).** Automated contributions into a boring,
  evidence-based allocation. No edge required, no edge attempted.
- **It's the risk ballast.** Businesses are concentrated, illiquid bets. The portfolio is
  diversified and liquid — it's what makes the whole plan survivable if a business fails.

**Confidence: 9/10** on the *mechanism*; market returns themselves are uncertain but we use
historically-grounded, low-cost, diversified vehicles rather than trying to beat the market.

## What we explicitly are NOT doing (and why)

| Rejected | Reason |
|---|---|
| Day trading / active crypto trading | Negative expected value after time + taxes; 15% success; violates "low labor." |
| Dropshipping / generic e-com | No moat, race-to-bottom margins, high time, 20% success. |
| Faceless content farms | Commoditized by AI itself; no durable advantage. |
| Generic "AI agency" (no vertical) | You'd compete with 10,000 others on price. The vertical is the moat. |
| Physical subscription box | High capital, logistics, low automation — opposite of your constraints. |

## Sequencing (the next 6 months)

1. **Months 1–2:** Launch Build 1. Land 1–2 paying clients. Stand up Build 3 with an initial
   contribution (even $100) so the habit/automation exists.
2. **Months 2–4:** Deliver, document every workflow, raise prices, get to 3–5 clients
   (~$6k–$10k/mo). Route first profits into Build 3 per the waterfall.
3. **Months 4–6:** Identify the #1 repeated workflow across clients → scope Build 2 (Micro-SaaS).
   Begin building it with service profit, not savings.
4. **Month 6+:** Run the continuous-improvement loop; let the capital-allocation waterfall route
   cash flow toward real estate and acquisitions as it grows.

# Outreach Batch #1 — Ready to Send

Ten drafts grouped by who you're talking to. Each is written to send **today** — just fill the
`[BRACKETS]` (name + one specific, real detail) and hit send. Keep them short; do not add a brochure.

**Rules that make these work:**
- One specific, true detail per message (a project you shared, a complaint they made). Generic = ignored.
- One soft ask: a 15-minute call. Never attach anything on the first touch.
- Send from your real personal email/phone, not a marketing blast tool.
- Log each send in the CRM and set `Next Action Date` = +3 business days.

---

## A) Close contacts / former colleagues (highest hit rate — start here)

**A1 — text or email**
> [First name] — quick one. I've started building automation systems that take the back-office
> grind off contractors — bid follow-ups, chasing overdue invoices, submittal/COI tracking. Thought
> of you because of [the office headaches you mentioned on the [Project] job]. Worth 15 min to see if
> it'd save you real time? No pitch — if it's not a fit I'll say so.

**A2 — for someone who runs a trade business**
> Hey [First name] — I left the capital-projects world to build automation for contractors. Just the
> repetitive office stuff that eats nights and weekends. Given [Company] is [growing / busy as hell],
> figured it might be useful. Got 15 min this week for me to show you one thing I'd automate for you?

---

## B) Trade contractor owners (warm-ish / referred)

**B1 — lead with lost-bid pain (best opener for owners)**
> [First name] — I build automation for [trade] contractors. The one that usually lands hardest:
> most shops lose jobs not on price but because nobody follows up on the quote. I set up a system
> that auto-follows-up on every bid and flags the hot ones. For a shop doing [X] bids a month it
> typically recovers a few jobs that were just going cold. Worth a 15-min look?

**B2 — lead with collections**
> [First name] — quick question for [Company]: how much time goes into chasing overdue invoices? I
> automate that — polite, escalating reminders that go out on their own, plus a short "needs a call"
> list for the ones that matter. Usually pays for itself in recovered cash the first month. Open to a
> 15-min walkthrough?

## C) Office managers / owners' spouses who run the back office (underrated buyers)

**C1**
> Hi [First name] — I build automation that takes the repetitive paperwork off whoever runs the
> office at a contracting business — bid follow-ups, invoice reminders, tracking submittals and
> insurance certs so nothing slips. I know how much of that lands on one person. Could I show you one
> piece I'd automate, in 15 minutes? No software for you to learn — it just runs.

## D) General contractors / PMs (your peer group)

## E) Suppliers / vendors (not buyers — referral multipliers)

**E1 — partnership angle (they know every contractor in town)**

> goodwill for [Supplier], useful for your customers. Worth a quick chat?

## F) Cold-but-credible (use only with a real, specific hook + a proof point once you have one)

**F1 — LinkedIn DM / email**

> now I automate the back office for [trade] contractors. [Once you have a result, insert it here:

# 04 — Capital Allocation Waterfall

> How every dollar of profit is recycled toward financial independence. Spreadsheet: `models/capital-allocation-waterfall.csv`.

---

## 1. The principle

Business profit is **not** spending money. It is fuel. Left as cash it does nothing; deployed on a disciplined waterfall it compounds into freedom. The waterfall answers one question automatically so you never agonize over it: *"I just made a dollar of profit — where does it go?"*

The allocation **shifts by stage**. Early, almost everything is reinvested into automation that buys back your time. As cash flow matures, the dominant sink shifts to the passive engine and then to durable ownership — mirroring the L1→L3→L4 progression in the strategy doc.

---

## 2. The waterfall, by stage

Percentages apply to **net profit after the tax set-aside** unless noted. Tax is always skimmed first.

| Stage | Monthly net profit | Tax | Biz reserve | Reinvest growth | Passive (L3) | Ownership (L4) | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Pre-revenue** | $0 | — | — | — | — | — | Validate. Spend only within the $500 cap. |
| **1 — First cash** | $0–2k | 30% | 20% | 50% | 0% | 0% | Fill a 3–6 mo business reserve; pour into automation that cuts *your* hours. |
| **2 — Traction** | $2–5k | 30% | 10% | 30% | 30% | 0% | Reserve full; start feeding the passive engine on the funding order. |
| **3 — Scaling** | $5–10k | 30% | 5% | 20% | 40% | 5% | Passive engine becomes the primary sink; seed an ownership fund. |
| **4 — Cash machine** | $10–25k | 30% | 5% | 10% | 35% | 20% | Build a real-estate / acquisition war chest while compounding L3. |
| **5 — Family office** | $25k+ | 30% | 5% | 5% | 30% | 30% | Deploy into durable ownership; passive income nears expense coverage. |

*The 30% tax set-aside is a placeholder — your real effective rate depends on entity structure and state. Confirm with a CPA and adjust the model.*

## 3. The fixed priority *within* each stage

Even inside a stage, dollars fill these buckets in strict order (a dollar never skips a higher-priority bucket):

```
1. TAX set-aside (skim first, always)              ← never touch; remit on schedule
2. BUSINESS operating reserve (3–6 mo opex)        ← survival; fill before any distribution
3. PERSONAL emergency fund (6 mo expenses)         ← stability
4. HIGH-INTEREST DEBT payoff (>~7%)                ← guaranteed return
5. REINVEST in growth/automation (capped per stage)← buys back your time
6. PASSIVE ENGINE (L3, on the account-funding order)← compounding core
7. OWNERSHIP (L4: real estate / acquisition)       ← durable assets, last
```

This ordering encodes "don't grow a fragile business" — reserves and personal stability come before growth bets, and growth bets come before passive deployment. Only genuine surplus reaches L4.

## 4. The full recycling loop (your stated allocation chain, made concrete)

```
   Income ──▶ Automation ──▶ More capacity ──▶ More income
      │                                            │
      └──────────────▶ Passive Engine (L3) ◀───────┘
                            │
                            ▼
                Real Estate + SMB Acquisition (L4)
                            │
                            ▼
                 Passive income ≥ expenses  =  FINANCIAL INDEPENDENCE
```

Each arrow is governed by the table in §2. The loop is *dynamic*: when a better opportunity appears (a property at the right cap rate, a business at <3× SDE), the improvement loop (`docs/05`) flags it and you can temporarily over-weight L4 — but only with surplus that has already cleared buckets 1–4.
